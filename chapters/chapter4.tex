\chapter{Methodology}

\section{Chapter Introduction}


This chapter presents the research methodology used to design and evaluate the proposed semantic enrichment
model for heterogeneous internship postings. Building on the theoretical foundations discussed in Chapter 2 and
the technological environment described in Chapter 3, the chapter defines how noisy internship and job-posting
data are transformed into structured, interpretable, and discovery-ready records.
The chapter does not treat the prototype application as the main research contribution. Instead, the prototype is
used as the methodological artifact through which the proposed semantic structuring approach is implemented,
observed, and evaluated. The research focus is the transformation of heterogeneous internship postings into a
consistent representation that can support internship-oriented information retrieval beyond keyword-based
search.
The methodology is organized around five connected concerns. First, the chapter defines the research design
used to connect the research questions to the implemented artifact. Second, it explains how heterogeneous
internship data are inspected and represented as a research problem. Third, it presents the semantic structuring
model, including deterministic normalization, controlled skill and category representation, embedding-based
semantic interpretation, and uncertainty-aware review handling. Fourth, it defines the structured internship
representation used as the target output of the enrichment process. Finally, it establishes the evaluation
methodology used to compare the proposed model with simpler keyword-oriented and deterministic
configurations.
The lightweight nature of the system is treated as a design constraint and evaluation condition. It remains
important because the proposed approach should be practical and reproducible, but it is not the central research
problem. The central problem is how heterogeneous internship postings can be semantically structured to
support reliable, interpretable, and internship-oriented discovery.


\section{Research Design}


This thesis follows a design-oriented empirical research approach. The study begins from a practical
information-management problem: internship postings collected from online recruitment environments are semistructured, lexically inconsistent, semantically ambiguous, and frequently mixed with non-internship
opportunities. To investigate this problem, the thesis designs a semantic enrichment model, implements it in a
prototype system, and evaluates its behavior on noisy internship and job-posting data.
The research design is appropriate because the thesis concerns both representation and system behavior. The
proposed model cannot be evaluated only by describing its architecture; it must be examined through the quality
of the records it produces, the discovery behavior it supports, the uncertainty signals it exposes, and the
limitations it demonstrates under noisy real-world posting conditions.
The methodological logic of the thesis follows four stages:
9. Problem analysis: identify structural, lexical, semantic, and quality-related inconsistencies in heterogeneous
internship postings.
10. Model design: define a lightweight semantic enrichment model that combines deterministic normalization,
controlled vocabulary representation, embedding-based semantic interpretation, and uncertainty handling.
11. Prototype implementation: implement the model as an internship aggregation and discovery artifact that
supports ingestion, enrichment, storage, and browsing.
12. Empirical evaluation: compare the enriched system against deterministic and keyword-oriented baselines,
while examining discovery effectiveness, trustworthiness, and operational boundaries.


In this design, the software system functions as an experimental vehicle. It allows the proposed semantic
structuring model to be tested using concrete data transformations, measurable outputs, and observable
discovery behavior. Therefore, system architecture, schema design, and pipeline implementation are discussed
in this chapter only insofar as they support the investigation of the research questions.

\subsection{Unified Internship Schema Definition}

To achieve a unified representation of heterogeneous recruitment data, the system requires a clearly defined target schema. External internship data originates from multiple sources, including semi-structured scraped web content and externally supplied JSON datasets with inconsistent field names and formatting. Direct frontend use of such raw data is impractical without cleaning, validation, and structural transformation. The target schema therefore defines the common structure into which raw records are transformed during the enrichment pipeline \cite{sarawagi2008information}.
The schema is not only a storage format. It also acts as a shared data contract between preprocessing, semantic enrichment, persistence, API delivery, and frontend presentation. By enforcing a consistent representation, the system reduces ambiguity and limits the propagation of data inconsistencies across pipeline stages \cite{chu2016data}.
Schema Design Requirements
The schema design is motivated by three main requirements.
Record Identity and Source Traceability
Aggregated recruitment data may contain duplicate or near-duplicate records across scraping cycles. To support stable identification and dataset consistency, each processed internship record requires a unique identifier. When reliable source identifiers are available, they may be preserved; otherwise, stable identifiers are generated from normalized record metadata such as title, company, and location. Source traceability is also preserved through the original listing URL and source platform information, supporting auditing and provenance tracking \cite{christen2012data}.
Discovery-Oriented Metadata
The frontend application requires structured metadata that allows users to browse, filter, and inspect internship listings efficiently. The schema therefore includes standardized attributes such as category labels, extracted skills, location, salary, company information, and cleaned descriptions. These fields support practical filtering, consistent frontend presentation, and student-facing discovery \cite{manning2008introduction}.
Cross-Layer Consistency
The schema must support interoperability between preprocessing logic, semantic enrichment, JSON persistence, API delivery, and frontend rendering. A consistent structured entity reduces confusion between independently developed components and improves maintainability \cite{chu2016data}.
Internship Schema as an Application Contract
These requirements are implemented through the final internship record structure consumed by the web application. The Python enrichment pipeline produces records compatible with this schema, while the TypeScript Internship interface enforces the expected structure inside the Next.js frontend.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Principal fields of the Internship schema}]
id: string -- deterministic unique identifier generated from normalized internship metadata

link: string -- original internship source URL

Structured Discovery Attributes

category: string -- application-facing internship domain category

skills: string[] -- extracted skill labels

location: string -- normalized location metadata

salary: string -- cleaned salary representation

Additional Metadata

title: string -- normalized internship title

description: string -- cleaned internship description

company: string -- employer name

job\_age: string -- posting age or publication timing metadata

platform: string -- originating source platform

rating: string -- employer rating where available

duration?: string -- optional internship duration metadata
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Taxonomy Selection: The ESCO Framework}

The European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations (ESCO) framework provides a standardized multilingual classification system designed to describe occupations, skills, competences, and qualifications within the European labor market \cite{esco2024}. ESCO is widely used in recruitment informatics and labor-market analytics because it provides a structured vocabulary for reducing ambiguity in professional terminology and improving interoperability between heterogeneous employment datasets \cite{giabelli2021skills, pappas2022jobbert}.

ESCO separates labor-market knowledge into interrelated conceptual pillars, most notably occupations and skills. This structured decomposition makes it particularly relevant when processing internship and job listing data, where employers frequently describe equivalent competencies using inconsistent terminology. For example, postings may refer to ``frontend engineering,'' ``UI development,'' or ``React development'' while describing closely related professional skill areas.

Although the proposed system does not implement full ontology-level ESCO occupation mapping, the framework serves as an important conceptual reference for the semantic enrichment methodology. Specifically, ESCO informed the design of the internal categorization logic and the construction of curated JSON-based skill reference libraries used during heuristic normalization and semantic enrichment.

This inspiration is particularly relevant for skill normalization. Recruitment data often contains vocabulary inconsistency, abbreviations, overlapping terminology, and informal naming conventions that reduce structured comparability. By adopting the general principle of controlled vocabulary normalization inspired by ESCO, the system improves consistency in extracted skill labeling while avoiding the implementation complexity of full taxonomy alignment.

ESCO is therefore used in this research as a semantic design reference rather than a strict operational dependency. This approach preserves the benefits of standardized recruitment terminology while remaining compatible with the thesis objective of lightweight, practical semantic enrichment on commodity infrastructure.

\subsection{Internal Categorization and Mapping}

While ESCO provides a useful conceptual reference for standardized occupational terminology, the proposed system relies on an internally defined categorization framework better aligned with the practical requirements of lightweight semantic enrichment and frontend usability.

A full ontology-driven occupation hierarchy would introduce unnecessary implementation complexity for the scope of this system, particularly given the variability and ambiguity of internship data originating from heterogeneous external sources. Instead, the enrichment pipeline uses curated internal JSON-based semantic reference libraries that support practical classification, skill normalization, and structured filtering.

This internal categorization strategy is informed by ESCO's principle of controlled vocabulary normalization but is intentionally simplified to support lightweight deployment and maintainable domain adaptation.

The categorization framework is implemented through curated semantic reference resources including domain keyword mappings, internship classification rules, negative role exclusion patterns, and structured skill libraries.

The internal domain space includes categories spanning multiple professional areas, including software engineering, data science, cybersecurity, cloud and DevOps, engineering, healthcare, finance, marketing, operations, sales, human resources, research, education, legal, manufacturing, and related professional domains.

This broader categorization approach provides several methodological advantages:

Practical semantic normalization:

Equivalent or closely related employer terminology can be mapped into consistent internal categories despite vocabulary inconsistency.

Frontend filtering compatibility:

Structured categories support user-facing filtering and browsing functionality without exposing complex external taxonomy structures.

Lightweight maintainability:

JSON-based semantic reference libraries can be updated incrementally as terminology evolves without requiring heavyweight ontology maintenance.

Flexible domain extensibility:

New industries, skill clusters, and internship categories can be introduced through controlled vocabulary updates rather than architectural redesign.

This internal semantic mapping strategy therefore balances conceptual standardization with implementation practicality, directly supporting the lightweight design objectives of the proposed system.

\subsection{Addressing the Semantic Gap}

A central challenge in heterogeneous recruitment data processing is the semantic gap between inconsistent employer terminology and the structured representations required for reliable internship discovery. Employers frequently describe equivalent roles, competencies, and technologies using highly variable language, abbreviations, informal naming conventions, or domain-specific jargon. Without normalization, this variability reduces dataset consistency, complicates filtering, and weakens structured comparability across listings \cite{abiteboul1997querying, sarawagi2008information}.

The proposed system addresses this semantic gap through a layered semantic normalization strategy informed by both standardized recruitment terminology principles and practical lightweight enrichment methods.

The first level of this strategy is controlled semantic normalization. Inspired by frameworks such as ESCO, the system reduces vocabulary inconsistency by aligning heterogeneous employer terminology with internally curated semantic reference libraries \cite{esco2024, senger2024deep}. This improves consistency in extracted skills, domain categorization, and structured metadata generation without requiring full ontology-level taxonomy enforcement.

The second level is internal categorization and enrichment abstraction. Raw internship and job descriptions often contain highly specific or inconsistent terminology unsuitable for direct frontend presentation or structured filtering. The enrichment pipeline therefore transforms noisy source language into stable internal categories and normalized skill labels better suited for application-level discovery and user-facing browsing \cite{chu2016data, sarawagi2008information}.

The third level is context-aware semantic classification through lightweight transformer inference. Deterministic heuristics alone cannot reliably interpret ambiguous terminology or context-dependent internship descriptions. The semantic classification layer therefore provides contextual interpretation where rule-based normalization is insufficient, improving classification robustness for noisy or semantically unclear postings \cite{reimers2019sentence, schwartz2020green}.

Rather than addressing the semantic gap through expensive live query-time semantic retrieval, the proposed methodology resolves semantic inconsistency during scheduled enrichment and dataset transformation. This shifts computational complexity away from user interaction and supports the thesis objective of lightweight, practical semantic processing on commodity hardware \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}

This section defines the framework for empirically evaluating the proposed internship aggregation and semantic enrichment system. The evaluation methodology examines extraction reliability, semantic normalization effectiveness, computational efficiency, and practical operational performance under commodity hardware constraints.

The evaluation design is grounded in established principles of Natural Language Processing (NLP), information extraction, and software systems evaluation \cite{chu2016data, sarawagi2008information, voorhees2005trec}. Particular emphasis is placed on evaluation validity, ensuring that the chosen measurements accurately reflect the semantic processing quality and practical system behavior being investigated \cite{voorhees2005trec}.

The methodology formalizes the evaluation objectives, links them to the research questions, defines the reference dataset and benchmarking conditions, introduces both quantitative and qualitative assessment criteria, and establishes the experimental configurations necessary to examine the trade-off between semantic effectiveness and computational cost.

Because the thesis explicitly targets lightweight practical deployment rather than large-scale cloud inference, the evaluation additionally considers computational efficiency and resource practicality in alignment with Green AI principles \cite{menghani2023efficient}.

\subsection{Evaluation Objectives}

The evaluation is designed to assess both the semantic output quality and the practical operational characteristics of the proposed internship aggregation and enrichment system. The primary objective is to determine whether the proposed lightweight three-layer architecture can achieve meaningful semantic enrichment and internship classification while remaining computationally practical for deployment on commodity hardware.

The evaluation is explicitly structured around the four research questions defined in Chapter 1, with each question mapped to measurable experimental objectives grounded in prior literature on information extraction, semantic processing, and efficient machine learning systems \cite{bruch2023analysis, schwartz2020green, sarawagi2008information, voorhees2005trec}.

RQ1 (Architecture Efficiency--Quality Balance)

How can a lightweight semantic enrichment pipeline balance internship classification quality with computational efficiency on commodity hardware?

This objective evaluates the overall effectiveness of the proposed three-layer architecture by examining the trade-off between semantic processing quality and practical computational cost. Measurements include enrichment consistency, runtime practicality, memory behavior, and frontend responsiveness under realistic operating conditions. This directly tests the thesis claim that meaningful semantic enrichment can be achieved without heavyweight infrastructure or cloud-dependent inference \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

RQ2 (Sentence-Transformer Classification Effectiveness)

How effectively can lightweight sentence-transformer models support internship relevance classification for noisy internship and job postings?

This objective evaluates the contribution of the transformer-based semantic classification layer when handling ambiguous or noisy recruitment data that cannot be reliably resolved through deterministic heuristics alone. The evaluation focuses on internship relevance classification quality, semantic ambiguity resolution, and practical inference behavior using lightweight sentence-transformer models \cite{reimers2019sentence, schwartz2020green}.

RQ3 (Semantic Enrichment Usability Improvement)

How effectively can semantic enrichment techniques improve the usability of internship records collected from heterogeneous online sources?

This objective evaluates whether semantic enrichment improves the practical usability of aggregated internship data by reducing vocabulary inconsistency, improving structured categorization, supporting skill extraction, and producing cleaner frontend-consumable internship records. The focus is on semantic consistency, dataset usability, and structured enrichment effectiveness rather than live retrieval benchmarking \cite{chu2016data, sarawagi2008information, doan2012principles}.

RQ4 (Heuristic Normalization Automation)

Which heuristic-based normalization strategies can automate the ingestion of inconsistently structured internship data into a unified schema?

This objective evaluates the effectiveness of deterministic preprocessing and heuristic normalization for transforming heterogeneous raw recruitment data into a structured canonical schema. Particular emphasis is placed on structural validation, malformed record filtering, metadata normalization, duplicate handling support, and deterministic attribute extraction \cite{doan2012principles, ji2023survey, sarawagi2008information}.

\subsection{Ground Truth Construction}

A reliable evaluation reference is necessary to assess the semantic processing quality and practical behavior of the proposed system. Because the thesis focuses on lightweight applied system development rather than large-scale benchmark creation, the evaluation uses a manually inspected representative reference subset rather than a formal externally annotated benchmark corpus.

The reference evaluation process was designed to support qualitative correctness assessment, structured output verification, comparative pipeline analysis, and practical performance observation \cite{christen2012data, sarawagi2008information, voorhees2005trec}.

Evaluation Dataset and Reference Construction

The evaluation is based on internship and job listing data processed through the implemented enrichment pipeline. The underlying dataset consists of heterogeneous recruitment records collected from multiple online sources, including scraped internship listings and structured JSON-based partner data with varying schema quality.

A representative evaluation subset was manually inspected to assess semantic correctness and structured output quality. The sampling process intentionally included diverse posting types, ambiguous internship descriptions, clearly irrelevant professional roles, and heterogeneous metadata quality to ensure meaningful evaluation coverage.

This manually reviewed subset served as the practical reference point for evaluating:

\begin{itemize}
\item heuristic normalization correctness
\item internship relevance classification behavior
\item semantic enrichment consistency
\item structured schema completeness
\item ambiguity handling and manual-review flagging behavior
\end{itemize}

Manual Semantic Verification

Because the thesis evaluates a practical applied enrichment system rather than a formal benchmark competition model, semantic correctness assessment was performed through structured manual inspection of processed outputs.

This review focused on comparing raw recruitment records against enriched outputs to determine whether:

\begin{itemize}
\item irrelevant non-internship postings were correctly excluded
\item ambiguous internship listings were semantically interpreted appropriately
\item extracted skills aligned with visible listing content
\item domain categorization remained semantically coherent
\item normalized metadata fields preserved meaningful information
\end{itemize}

This manual verification process supports qualitative evaluation of semantic enrichment effectiveness and classification robustness \cite{sarawagi2008information, chu2016data}.

Comparative Pipeline Assessment

To isolate the contribution of semantic classification, comparative observation was conducted between deterministic heuristic preprocessing alone and the combined heuristic + transformer-assisted enrichment pipeline.

This comparison supports evaluation of:

\begin{itemize}
\item classification improvement for ambiguous listings
\item reduction in false positive internship acceptance
\item improved semantic interpretation beyond keyword heuristics
\item practical trade-offs between inference cost and enrichment quality
\end{itemize}

This comparative design directly supports the evaluation of RQ1 and RQ2.

Operational Performance Reference Measurements

Practical performance evaluation was conducted through direct runtime observation during local execution.

Measured characteristics include:

\begin{itemize}
\item end-to-end enrichment runtime
\item approximate processing throughput
\item semantic pipeline execution practicality
\item frontend interaction responsiveness measured through Chromium DevTools
\item cache-assisted browsing responsiveness after initial frontend data loading
\end{itemize}

Because the thesis emphasizes lightweight deployment practicality, these operational observations serve as an important complement to semantic correctness evaluation \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

\subsection{Metrics}

The evaluation employs a multi-dimensional set of quantitative and qualitative metrics to assess both semantic output quality and operational practicality. Because the proposed system combines deterministic heuristics, lightweight semantic classification, and applied frontend deployment, no single metric is sufficient to capture overall system effectiveness \cite{chu2016data, bruch2023analysis, voorhees2005trec}.

Extraction and Classification Quality

For evaluating structured normalization and semantic classification behavior (RQ2 and RQ4), the study applies standard correctness-oriented assessment criteria.

Precision and Recall:

Precision and recall are used where applicable to assess the trade-off between false positive and false negative classification behavior, particularly when comparing heuristic filtering against transformer-assisted semantic classification \cite{robertson2009probabilistic}.

F1-Score:

The harmonic mean of precision and recall is used as a balanced summary metric when evaluating internship relevance classification performance, particularly in scenarios where both false acceptance and false rejection are important.

Manual Semantic Correctness Assessment:

Because portions of the evaluation rely on structured manual inspection rather than formal benchmark datasets, qualitative semantic correctness is also assessed through expert review of processed internship outputs. This includes correctness of domain categorization, internship relevance interpretation, and skill extraction consistency.

Semantic Enrichment and Dataset Quality

For evaluating the usability improvements introduced by semantic enrichment (RQ3), the study assesses structured dataset quality through practical semantic consistency criteria.

Categorization Consistency:

Evaluation of whether semantically similar internship listings are consistently assigned to coherent internal domain categories.

Skill Extraction Quality:

Assessment of whether extracted skill labels accurately reflect visible internship content and remain semantically normalized across heterogeneous source terminology \cite{baeza2011modern, sarawagi2008information}.

Schema Completeness:

Assessment of the proportion of processed records successfully transformed into complete structured internship entities suitable for frontend deployment.

Ambiguity Handling Behavior:

Evaluation of whether uncertain or semantically ambiguous listings are appropriately flagged for manual review rather than incorrectly forced into confident automated classifications.

Operational Performance Metrics

For evaluating practical deployment feasibility (RQ1), the study includes lightweight operational performance measurements aligned with efficient NLP and Green AI evaluation principles \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

Pipeline Runtime Observation:

Total end-to-end semantic enrichment execution time observed during local pipeline runs.

Approximate Processing Throughput:

Estimated number of internship listings processed per unit time during enrichment execution.

Frontend Responsiveness:

Observed page loading, filtering, and interaction responsiveness measured through Chromium DevTools under realistic local browsing conditions.

Memory Practicality:

Approximate memory behavior observed during enrichment execution to assess compatibility with commodity hardware constraints.

Comparative Evaluation

To isolate the contribution of semantic classification, comparative evaluation is performed between:

deterministic heuristic preprocessing alone

heuristic preprocessing combined with transformer-assisted semantic classification

This comparison supports direct analysis of the trade-off between semantic effectiveness and computational cost central to the thesis research objectives.

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}

To attribute observed performance differences to specific architectural components, the evaluation adopts a comparative experimental methodology based on controlled system configurations. Comparative evaluation is a standard approach in software systems and machine learning experimentation for isolating the contribution of individual processing components within a larger pipeline [38, 59].

Baselines and Comparative Configurations

The evaluation compares multiple configurations representing increasing semantic capability and computational complexity.

Baseline 1: Deterministic Heuristic Processing (Layer A Only)

This configuration evaluates the deterministic preprocessing layer in isolation. It applies only explicit heuristic and rule-based processing mechanisms, including structural validation, malformed record filtering, metadata normalization, internship keyword heuristics, rule-based categorization, duplicate handling support, and deterministic skill extraction.

This baseline establishes the performance floor for the proposed architecture and evaluates how effectively heuristic normalization alone can process heterogeneous recruitment data without contextual semantic inference \cite{doan2012principles, ji2023survey, sarawagi2008information}.

Baseline 2: Full Lightweight Semantic Pipeline (Layer A + Layer B + Layer C)

This configuration represents the complete proposed methodology.

It combines:

Layer A: deterministic heuristic preprocessing and normalization

Layer B: lightweight sentence-transformer-based semantic internship relevance classification

Layer C: controlled manual-review exception handling for semantically ambiguous cases

This configuration serves as the primary experimental system and evaluates whether lightweight semantic classification improves internship relevance assessment, enrichment consistency, and overall dataset usability beyond deterministic preprocessing alone \cite{reimers2019sentence, schwartz2020green}.

The comparative difference between this configuration and the heuristic baseline isolates the practical contribution of transformer-assisted semantic enrichment.

Comparative Reference: General-Purpose LLM Assessment

A limited exploratory comparative assessment was conducted using general-purpose large language models, including ChatGPT and DeepSeek, to provide contextual comparison against contemporary cloud-based semantic reasoning systems.

This comparison was exploratory rather than a formal benchmark and was not intended as a statistically controlled production evaluation. Instead, it served as supplementary qualitative reference for examining differences in:

internship relevance classification behavior

semantic interpretation plausibility

enrichment consistency

explainability characteristics

practical computational and infrastructure implications

Because the thesis explicitly targets lightweight local deployment, this comparative reference helps contextualize whether useful semantic enrichment can be achieved without dependence on heavyweight cloud-hosted LLM inference \cite{eloundou2024gpts, schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

Implementation Details and Hardware Environment

To ensure reproducibility and fair operational comparison, all experiments were conducted on a single controlled local workstation representing the commodity hardware constraints defined in the thesis objectives.

Hardware configuration:

RAM: 16 GB

Storage: 715 GB total storage

GPU: None (CPU-only execution)

Operating System: Windows 11

Runtime Environment: local development execution environment

This configuration reflects the practical deployment assumptions central to the proposed lightweight semantic enrichment methodology, where meaningful semantic processing should remain feasible without dedicated accelerators or cloud-hosted inference infrastructure \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

Frontend Observation Environment

User-facing responsiveness was evaluated through practical interaction observation using Chromium browser developer tools during realistic local execution.

Observed interaction scenarios included:

initial internship catalog loading

cached repeated browsing behavior

frontend filtering responsiveness

internship detail exploration

authenticated interaction flows where applicable

These observations support practical usability evaluation rather than formal production-scale stress testing.

Comparative Trade-Off Analysis

The experimental design explicitly examines the trade-off between semantic effectiveness and computational practicality.

The comparison between deterministic preprocessing, the full lightweight semantic pipeline, and exploratory LLM reference assessment allows investigation of the thesis's central architectural question: whether lightweight semantic enrichment can provide meaningful classification and enrichment capability while maintaining practical operational efficiency on commodity hardware.

This comparative framing aligns directly with the Green AI objective of reducing computational cost while preserving useful semantic capability \cite{menghani2023efficient}.

\section{Chapter Conclusion}

This chapter has defined the methodological framework of the thesis. It established the functional requirements, architectural design, semantic processing methodology, structured data schema, internal categorization strategy, and evaluation framework used to assess the proposed internship aggregation and enrichment system.

The chapter described the lightweight three-layer semantic processing architecture, including deterministic heuristic preprocessing, transformer-assisted semantic classification, and controlled manual-review handling for ambiguous cases. It also formalized the structured data representation used throughout the system and explained how semantic normalization improves the usability of heterogeneous internship data collected from multiple sources.

The evaluation methodology defined in this chapter established the experimental objectives, qualitative and quantitative assessment criteria, comparative configurations, and practical hardware environment used to assess both semantic effectiveness and operational feasibility.

These methodological decisions provide the foundation for the next chapter, which presents the concrete implementation of the ingestion, normalization, semantic enrichment, persistence, and frontend integration components.
