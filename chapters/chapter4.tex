\chapter{Methodology}

\section{Chapter Introduction}


This chapter presents the research methodology used to design and evaluate the proposed semantic enrichment
model for heterogeneous internship postings. Building on the theoretical foundations discussed in Chapter 2 and
the technological environment described in Chapter 3, the chapter defines how noisy internship and job-posting
data are transformed into structured, interpretable, and discovery-ready records \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design}.
The chapter does not treat the prototype application as the main research contribution. Instead, the prototype is
used as the methodological artifact through which the proposed semantic structuring approach is implemented,
observed, and evaluated \cite{hevner2004design}. The research focus is the transformation of heterogeneous internship postings into a
consistent representation that can support internship-oriented information retrieval beyond keyword-based
search \cite{manning2008introduction, baeza2011modern}.
The methodology is organized around five connected concerns. First, the chapter defines the research design
used to connect the research questions to the implemented artifact. Second, it explains how heterogeneous
internship data are inspected and represented as a research problem. Third, it presents the semantic structuring
model, including deterministic normalization, controlled skill and category representation, embedding-based
semantic interpretation, and uncertainty-aware review handling. Fourth, it defines the structured internship
representation used as the target output of the enrichment process. Finally, it establishes the evaluation
methodology used to compare the proposed model with simpler keyword-oriented and deterministic
configurations.
The lightweight nature of the system is treated as a design constraint and evaluation condition. It remains
important because the proposed approach should be practical and reproducible, but it is not the central research
problem. The central problem is how heterogeneous internship postings can be semantically structured to
support reliable, interpretable, and internship-oriented discovery \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.

\section{Research Design}

This thesis follows a design-oriented empirical research approach \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design}. The study begins from a practical information-management problem: internship postings collected from online recruitment environments are semi-structured, lexically inconsistent, semantically ambiguous, and frequently mixed with non-internship opportunities \cite{kurekova2015using, batini2009methodologies}. To investigate this problem, the thesis designs a semantic enrichment model, implements it in a prototype system, and evaluates its behavior on noisy internship and job-posting data.

The research design is appropriate because the thesis concerns both representation and system behavior. The proposed model cannot be evaluated only by describing its architecture; it must be examined through the quality of the records it produces, the discovery behavior it supports, the uncertainty signals it exposes, and the limitations it demonstrates under noisy real-world posting conditions \cite{peffers2007design, wohlin2012experimentation}.

The methodological logic of the thesis follows four stages:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Stage \arabic*:}, start=1, leftmargin=2.2cm]
    \item \textbf{Problem analysis:} identify structural, lexical, semantic, and quality-related inconsistencies in heterogeneous internship postings.

    \item \textbf{Model design:} define a lightweight semantic enrichment model that combines deterministic normalization, controlled vocabulary representation, embedding-based semantic interpretation, and uncertainty handling.

    \item \textbf{Prototype implementation:} implement the model as an internship aggregation and discovery artifact that supports ingestion, enrichment, storage, and browsing.

    \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation:} compare the enriched system against deterministic and keyword-oriented baselines, while examining discovery effectiveness, trustworthiness, and operational boundaries.
\end{enumerate}

In this design, the software system functions as an experimental vehicle. It allows the proposed semantic structuring model to be tested using concrete data transformations, measurable outputs, and observable discovery behavior \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design}. Therefore, system architecture, schema design, and pipeline implementation are discussed in this chapter only insofar as they support the investigation of the research questions.

\section{Dataset and Heterogeneity Analysis Strategy}


The first methodological task is to understand the nature of the data problem. The thesis focuses on internship
and job-posting records collected from heterogeneous online recruitment sources. These sources may include
general recruitment platforms, employer pages, and structured JSON datasets. Because many of these sources
are not dedicated internship platforms, collected records may contain genuine internships together with full-time
roles, graduate schemes, apprenticeships, training advertisements, and unrelated professional positions \cite{kurekova2015using}.
This creates a representation challenge before any frontend discovery can occur. The collected records must be
inspected, filtered, normalized, and semantically enriched before they can support reliable internship-oriented
retrieval. The methodology therefore treats heterogeneity as an empirical object of analysis rather than as a
simple preprocessing inconvenience \cite{batini2009methodologies, doan2012principles}.
The analysis considers four forms of inconsistency:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structural inconsistency:} differences in field names, missing values, inconsistent JSON shapes,
    incomplete metadata, or duplicated records.
    \item \textbf{Lexical inconsistency:} different words used for similar roles, skills, locations, or employment
    arrangements.
    \item \textbf{Semantic ambiguity:} postings that contain internship-related terms but do not clearly represent
    genuine internship opportunities, or postings that are valid internships but use non-standard wording.
    \item \textbf{Quality variation:} noisy descriptions, boilerplate text, residual markup, inconsistent salary
    formats, weak location information, and source-specific formatting artifacts.
\end{itemize}

This analysis supports RQ1 by establishing why internship discovery is not merely a keyword-search problem.
The same keywords may appear in irrelevant postings, while relevant internships may be described using
vocabulary that does not exactly match common student search terms. As a result, the methodology requires a
semantic structuring model that combines explainable rules with contextual semantic interpretation \cite{manning2008introduction, luan2021sparse}.

\section{Semantic Structuring Model}

The central methodological contribution of the thesis is the semantic structuring model. This model defines how
heterogeneous internship and job-posting records are transformed into normalized, semantically enriched, and
discovery-ready internship records \cite{sarawagi2008information, batini2009methodologies}.
The model is organized as a layered enrichment approach. The layers are not presented as isolated
implementation details, but as methodological responses to different kinds of data difficulty. Structurally
predictable inconsistencies are handled through deterministic normalization. Vocabulary variation is addressed
through controlled skill and category representations. Ambiguous internship relevance is addressed through
embedding-based semantic interpretation. Uncertain cases are represented through review indicators instead of
being forced into unreliable automated decisions \cite{reimers2019sentence, hunkenschroer2022ethics}.

\subsection{Deterministic Normalization Layer}

The first layer performs deterministic preprocessing and normalization. This layer is used for transformations
where rule-based methods are reliable, interpretable, and computationally inexpensive. Examples include field
validation, title cleanup, company and location normalization, salary pattern extraction, duplicate identifier
generation, and rejection of clearly malformed or irrelevant records \cite{sarawagi2008information, chu2016data}.
This layer supports structural consistency. It reduces noise before semantic interpretation begins and prevents
obvious low-quality records from propagating into the enriched dataset. Because the operations are
deterministic, they also support transparency: the reason for a transformation or rejection can usually be traced
to an explicit rule or pattern \cite{batini2009methodologies}.
Methodologically, this layer addresses the structural side of RQ1 and contributes to RQ2 by creating the
normalized foundation required for semantic enrichment. It also supports RQ5 because it keeps the model
lightweight and operationally feasible without depending on expensive inference for every transformation \cite{schwartz2020green, menghani2023efficient}.


\subsection{Controlled Skill and Category Representation}

The second part of the model represents skills and occupational domains using controlled internal vocabularies.
Online postings often describe the same skill or domain using different expressions. For example, related
programming roles may be described through job titles, technologies, frameworks, responsibilities, or employerspecific wording. Without controlled representation, these variations remain difficult to compare or filter
consistently \cite{peterson2001occupational, senger2024deep}.
The methodology therefore uses curated skill and category references to map noisy employer language into
stable internal labels. These labels are intended to support internship discovery, browsing, and filtering. They
are not intended to represent a complete occupational ontology or full labor-market taxonomy.
Occupational frameworks such as ESCO remain useful as conceptual references because they demonstrate the
value of standardized skill and occupation representation. However, full ESCO-level ontology alignment is
outside the scope of the thesis. The proposed approach instead uses a lighter internal categorization strategy
suitable for noisy internship data and student-centered discovery \cite{esco2024, staab2009handbook, shvaiko2013ontology}.
This component directly supports RQ2 by explaining how controlled vocabularies are combined with
normalization and embeddings. It also supports RQ3 because consistent skills and categories make the resulting
internship catalog more coherent and interpretable for discovery \cite{zhang2024nnose}.

\subsection{Embedding-Based Semantic Interpretation}

The third component addresses semantic ambiguity and lexical mismatch. Deterministic rules are useful for
explicit patterns, but they are limited when different postings express similar meanings through different
vocabulary. For instance, a genuine internship may be described as a placement, trainee role, student program,
or junior practical opportunity. Conversely, a full-time role may contain internship-related terms without being a
real internship.

To address this problem, the methodology incorporates lightweight embedding-based semantic interpretation.
Sentence-transformer representations are used to compare posting text against semantic reference patterns and to
support internship relevance classification \cite{reimers2019sentence, senger2024deep}. This allows the system to consider contextual similarity rather than
relying only on exact keyword overlap.
The embedding component is not treated as a black-box replacement for the rule-based layer. It operates
alongside deterministic normalization. This hybrid design improves robustness because explicit rules handle
high-confidence structural cases, while semantic embeddings support interpretation of ambiguous or lexically
varied postings \cite{luan2021sparse, sarawagi2008information}.
This component mainly supports RQ2, because it explains how embedding-based representations are integrated
into the enrichment model. It also supports RQ3 by improving discovery beyond keyword-oriented retrieval, and
RQ5 by demonstrating the limits of lightweight semantic inference when postings are vague or domain-diverse \cite{menghani2023efficient}.


\subsection{Uncertainty and Manual Review Handling}


The fourth component represents uncertainty instead of hiding it. Heterogeneous internship postings often
contain incomplete information, vague wording, conflicting signals, or ambiguous opportunity types. In such
cases, a system that forces every record into a confident real-internship or non-internship decision may create
unreliable outputs \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, rigotti2024fairness}.
The methodology therefore includes uncertainty-aware handling. Records may be marked for manual review
when confidence is low, when deterministic and semantic signals disagree, when the text is insufficiently
informative, or when a posting appears close to the boundary between internship and non-internship categories.
This review flag does not mean the system failed; rather, it indicates that the model has preserved uncertainty
instead of pretending that all cases can be automated safely \cite{krippendorff2004content}.
This design directly supports RQ4. It also improves interpretability because users and evaluators can distinguish
high-confidence automated results from ambiguous cases that require human inspection. For a student-centered
discovery system, this is important because unreliable classification could either hide genuine opportunities or
display irrelevant roles as internships \cite{raghavan2020mitigating}.

\section{Structured Internship Representation}

The target output of the methodology is a structured internship representation. This representation is not merely
a technical JSON format for frontend consumption. It is the semantic contract produced by the enrichment
process. Its purpose is to convert heterogeneous source postings into records that are consistent enough for
discovery, filtering, comparison, and evaluation \cite{doan2012principles, kleppmann2017designing}.

\subsection{Unified Internship Schema}

The unified schema defines the fields required for structured internship discovery. Core fields include stable
identifiers, title, company, location, source link, salary information, description, platform provenance, extracted
skills, category labels, duration information where available, internship probability, final internship decision,
and manual-review indicators \cite{doan2012principles, batini2009methodologies}.
The schema supports several methodological objectives. It preserves identity and provenance by linking
processed records back to their original sources. It supports normalized metadata by giving each record
consistent fields. It supports semantic discovery through skills, categories, and internship relevance signals. It
supports uncertainty management through confidence and review indicators. Finally, it supports evaluation by
making the outputs of normalization, enrichment, and classification inspectable \cite{christen2012data, krippendorff2004content}.
This schema is therefore central to the new research framing. It is the representation through which
heterogeneous postings become semantically structured internship records.

\subsection{Source Traceability and Metadata Consistency}

Because aggregated postings may come from multiple sources, traceability is necessary. The methodology
preserves platform information and original source links so that processed records can be connected back to
their raw listings. Stable identifiers also support deduplication and consistency across ingestion cycles \cite{christen2012data, elmagarmid2007duplicate}.
Metadata consistency is equally important. Fields such as title, company, location, salary, and category must be
normalized enough to support browsing and filtering. Inconsistent metadata reduces discovery quality even
when the underlying opportunity is relevant. For this reason, the methodology treats normalization and schema
completeness as part of semantic structuring rather than as minor formatting tasks \cite{batini2009methodologies, doan2012principles}.

\subsection{Taxonomy-Informed Internal Categorization}

The proposed category model is taxonomy-informed but not taxonomy-dependent. External frameworks such as
ESCO and O*NET provide useful conceptual foundations for thinking about occupations and skills, but the
thesis does not attempt full ontology-level alignment. Instead, the methodology uses internal categories that are
easier to maintain, easier to explain, and better suited to the scale and purpose of the prototype \cite{esco2024, peterson2001occupational, staab2009handbook}.
This strategy avoids overclaiming. The categories are designed for internship-oriented discovery and browsing,
not for complete labor-market analytics. The methodological value lies in creating enough semantic structure to
improve discovery while preserving transparency and manageable system complexity \cite{shvaiko2013ontology}.

\section{Processing Workflow}


The processing workflow operationalizes the semantic structuring model. It describes how raw postings move
through validation, normalization, enrichment, classification, uncertainty handling, storage, and frontend access \cite{peffers2007design, doan2012principles}.
The workflow begins with raw recruitment records obtained from heterogeneous sources. These records are first
validated to ensure that they contain enough information to be processed. Malformed records or records missing
essential identifiers are excluded from downstream processing. Valid records then pass through deterministic
cleaning and normalization, where titles, locations, salaries, descriptions, and other metadata are standardized as
far as possible \cite{chu2016data, batini2009methodologies}.
After normalization, the enrichment process extracts skills, assigns categories, generates stable identifiers,
detects duplicates, and prepares the record for internship relevance assessment. Ambiguous or lexically varied
postings are then evaluated using lightweight semantic interpretation. The final decision combines deterministic
and semantic evidence where appropriate \cite{reimers2019sentence, senger2024deep}.
Records with clear evidence are classified and exported into the structured internship catalog. Records with
insufficient or conflicting evidence are marked for manual review. This ensures that uncertainty remains visible.
The resulting catalog is then stored in a structured format for application-level discovery, while dynamic userspecific actions such as saving internships may be handled separately by the application persistence layer.
This workflow supports the MRQ because it transforms heterogeneous postings into semantically consistent,
interpretable, and discovery-ready representations. It also connects the methodology directly to Chapter 5, where
the concrete implementation of these workflow stages is described \cite{kleppmann2017designing}.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}

The evaluation methodology examines whether the proposed semantic enrichment model improves the
representation and discovery of internship postings compared with simpler approaches. The evaluation is
designed around the revised research questions and therefore considers data heterogeneity, enrichment behavior,
discovery effectiveness, uncertainty handling, and operational limitations \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, peffers2007design}.
The evaluation does not rely only on technical performance. Runtime, throughput, and hardware feasibility
remain relevant, but they are treated as boundary and feasibility indicators rather than as the central research
claim. The main evaluation question is whether the proposed model produces structured records that are more
reliable, interpretable, and useful for internship-oriented discovery than keyword-oriented or purely
deterministic alternatives \cite{voorhees2005trec, jain1991art}.

\subsection{Ground Truth Construction}

A manually inspected evaluation reference is required because internship relevance cannot be evaluated only from source metadata. The evaluation subset should include clear internships, clear non-internships, ambiguous cases, and records with inconsistent or incomplete metadata. This makes the reference useful for testing both classification correctness and uncertainty handling \cite{krippendorff2004content, wohlin2012experimentation}.

Manual inspection should compare the raw posting with the enriched output. The reviewer should check whether the final internship decision is reasonable, whether the skills reflect visible content, whether the category is coherent, whether salary and location information are preserved correctly, and whether ambiguous cases are properly marked for review \cite{krippendorff2004content}.

This approach is suitable for a Master thesis because the goal is not to create a large public benchmark. The goal is to evaluate whether the proposed semantic structuring model behaves reliably on representative noisy internship data. The manual reference also supports error analysis in Chapter 6 and discussion of validity in Chapter 7 \cite{wohlin2012experimentation}.

\subsection{Baseline Configurations}

The evaluation should compare the full semantic enrichment model against simpler alternatives. This helps isolate the added value of semantic structuring \cite{voorhees2005trec, wohlin2012experimentation}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Keyword-oriented baseline:} represents conventional lexical discovery, where relevance depends mainly on exact terms such as internship, intern, student, trainee, or placement.

    \item \textbf{Deterministic baseline:} uses rule-based normalization and heuristic filtering without embedding-based semantic interpretation.

    \item \textbf{Full semantic enrichment model:} combines deterministic normalization, controlled skill/category representation, embedding-based interpretation, and uncertainty-aware manual-review handling.

    \item \textbf{Exploratory LLM reference:} may be used as a contextual comparison, but it should be described as exploratory rather than as a formal production benchmark.
\end{itemize}

The comparison should show whether semantic enrichment improves discovery quality and representation coherence beyond simpler approaches. It should also show where the lightweight approach remains limited \cite{manning2008introduction, jain1991art}.

\subsection{Metrics}

The revised evaluation should use several categories of metrics because the thesis studies representation, discovery, uncertainty, and operational boundaries \cite{sokolova2009systematic, jarvelin2002cumulated, jain1991art}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Classification metrics:} precision, recall, F1-score, Top-1 accuracy, or Top-3 accuracy where applicable, used to assess internship relevance classification \cite{sokolova2009systematic}.

    \item \textbf{Discovery metrics:} Precision@5, Hit Rate@5, NDCG@10, or similar measures used to evaluate whether enriched records improve the ranking or surfacing of relevant internships \cite{manning2008introduction, jarvelin2002cumulated}.

    \item \textbf{Enrichment quality indicators:} schema completeness, skill extraction consistency, category coherence, salary normalization correctness, date extraction correctness, and metadata preservation \cite{batini2009methodologies}.

    \item \textbf{Uncertainty indicators:} proportion of manual-review cases, low-confidence decisions, rule/semantic disagreement cases, and ambiguous records correctly prevented from being overconfidently accepted \cite{krippendorff2004content}.

    \item \textbf{Operational feasibility indicators:} processing throughput, runtime, memory practicality, frontend responsiveness, and cost or dependency comparison with heavier LLM-based processing \cite{jain1991art, schwartz2020green}.
\end{itemize}

The word usability should be used carefully. Unless a formal user study is conducted with students or real users, the evaluation should avoid strong claims about user experience. The more accurate terms are discovery effectiveness, structured discovery quality, coherence, and interpretability.

\subsection{Operational Feasibility Measures}

Operational feasibility remains part of the evaluation, but its role changes under the new research framing. It should no longer be presented as the main research question. Instead, it supports RQ5 by identifying the boundary conditions of lightweight semantic enrichment \cite{jain1991art, menghani2023efficient}.

The evaluation may therefore report local execution time, approximate listings processed per second, CPU-only feasibility, frontend loading behavior, and cost comparison with external LLM-based workflows. These measures help demonstrate whether the proposed model can remain practical under realistic student-project or small-platform constraints \cite{schwartz2020green, jain1991art}.

However, these operational measures should be interpreted as supporting evidence. The primary intellectual claim remains that semantic structuring can improve the representation and discovery of heterogeneous internship postings while preserving interpretability and manageable complexity \cite{peffers2007design}.


\section{Chapter Conclusion}

This chapter has defined the revised methodology of the thesis. It reframed the methodology around the semantic structuring of heterogeneous internship postings rather than around the software artifact alone. The chapter explained the design-oriented empirical research approach, the analysis of data heterogeneity, the layered semantic enrichment model, the structured internship representation, the processing workflow, and the evaluation design \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design}.

The proposed methodology combines deterministic normalization, controlled skill and category representation, lightweight embedding-based semantic interpretation, and uncertainty-aware manual-review handling. This combination is designed to transform noisy internship and job-posting data into structured records that are more consistent, interpretable, and suitable for internship-oriented discovery \cite{sarawagi2008information, reimers2019sentence, krippendorff2004content}.

The evaluation framework defined in this chapter aligns the methodology with the revised main research question and RQ1--RQ5. It examines heterogeneity, semantic structuring behavior, discovery effectiveness, uncertainty handling, and operational boundaries \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, sokolova2009systematic, jarvelin2002cumulated}. These methodological decisions provide the foundation for Chapter 5, which describes the concrete implementation of the enrichment model, and Chapter 6, which evaluates its empirical behavior.
