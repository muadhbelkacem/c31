\chapter{General Introduction}

\section{Chapter Introduction}
This chapter introduces the research problem addressed in this thesis and defines its academic scope. It presents the context of modern internship discovery, highlights the limitations of traditional keyword-based job search when dealing with noisy and inconsistent recruitment data, and states the research questions guiding this work. It also summarizes the main contributions of the proposed system and outlines the structure of the remaining chapters.

\section{Context and Motivation}
Over the last decade, the process of internship discovery has been fundamentally transformed by the digital shift in the global labor market \cite{faliagka2012online}. Traditional channels, such as physical noticeboards and newspaper classifieds, have been superseded by a diverse ecosystem of online platforms. Students now navigate a complex landscape of global platforms like LinkedIn and Indeed, specialized university career portals, and niche industry-specific boards. While this shift has significantly expanded access to a high volume of opportunities \cite{kurekova2015using}, it has also introduced a formidable data-management challenge.

Internship listings are frequently semi-structured, inconsistent, and expressed through platform-specific conventions. Two different employers may describe essentially identical roles using disparate terminology; for instance, a ``Junior Java Developer'' and a ``Software Engineering Intern'' might require the same core competencies, yet traditional keyword searches would treat them as distinct entities. Moreover, the data is often noisy, containing boilerplate text, inconsistent formatting, and non-standard labels \cite{tamburri2020data}. For recruitment platforms and educational institutions, organizing this continuous stream of unstructured data from automated web scraping and manual uploads is a resource-intensive task. This challenge motivates the need for a lightweight, scalable approach that can transform noisy listings into a structured semantic knowledge base, enabling precise discovery without the overhead of manual curation or expensive computational infrastructure \cite{boselli2018classifying}.

\section{Problem Statement}
This thesis addresses two critical challenges in the ingestion and retrieval of internship data: the semantic gap and structural heterogeneity. Existing job-posting systems generally adopt one of two approaches, both of which present significant limitations for large-scale, cost-effective deployment:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em,topsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{Rigid Lexical Matching:} Many platforms rely on exact keyword matching, which fails to account for synonyms, technical jargon, and the underlying relationships between professional concepts. Consequently, users often miss relevant listings because their search terms do not precisely align with the employer's specific vocabulary \cite{baeza2011modern}.
\item \textbf{High-Cost LLM Dependency:} Conversely, some systems delegate the entire extraction and retrieval problem to Large Language Models (LLMs). While LLMs are highly effective at interpreting meaning, relying on them to process thousands of scraped documents in real-time is often financially prohibitive and introduces substantial latency \cite{menghani2023efficient}.
\end{itemize}

Furthermore, the ingestion process itself is fragmented. Data from various partner institutions often arrives in heterogeneous formats lacking a fixed schema, necessitating repetitive manual mapping. This creates a clear need for a unified system that can automatically clean, structure, and semantically enrich data from diverse sources while remaining efficient enough to operate on standard commodity hardware \cite{giabelli2021skills}.

\section{Research Questions}
This study is guided by the following four research questions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em,topsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How can a lightweight semantic enrichment pipeline balance internship classification quality with computational efficiency on commodity hardware?
\item \textbf{RQ2:} How effectively can lightweight sentence-transformer models support internship relevance classification for noisy internship and job postings?
\item \textbf{RQ3:} How effectively can semantic enrichment techniques improve the usability of internship records collected from heterogeneous online sources?
\item \textbf{RQ4:} Which heuristic-based normalization strategies can automate the ingestion of inconsistently structured internship data into a unified schema?
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}
The primary contributions of this thesis are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em,topsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{A Tiered Enrichment Architecture:} Introduction of a modular architecture that begins with deterministic rule-based extraction and defines extension points for lightweight neural mapping and resource-budgeted LLM fallback in complex cases.
\item \textbf{A Structured Internship Catalog Prototype:} Implementation of a Next.js and TypeScript system that loads a JSON internship catalog, normalizes key fields, extracts skill and category metadata, exposes API routes, and presents records through searchable role pages.
\item \textbf{Automated Ingestion Heuristics:} Development of lightweight JSON processing and validation heuristics that reduce manual cleanup for heterogeneous internship records.
\item \textbf{Evaluation Framework:} An evaluation design using a real-world dataset of 327 listings, documenting how extraction quality, retrieval behavior, and computational performance should be measured as the semantic layers mature.
\end{itemize}

\section{Thesis Structure}
The thesis is organized into seven chapters. Chapter 1 introduces the research context, problem statement, research questions, and primary contributions. Chapter 2 provides the theoretical foundations and reviews related work in information extraction, occupational taxonomies, and semantic retrieval. Chapter 3 describes the technological environment and development tools used in the implementation, including the Next.js framework and transformer-based semantic tools. Chapter 4 presents the methodology, defining the system architecture, semantic schema, and evaluation protocol. Chapter 5 details the system design and implementation, covering the data ingestion, enrichment, and storage layers. Chapter 6 presents the evaluation results, including a detailed analysis of $HR_k$ and $NDCG_k$ metrics and a comparison of retrieval strategies. Chapter 7 concludes the thesis by discussing the findings, addressing ethical and privacy considerations, and outlining directions for future work.

\section{Chapter Conclusion}
This chapter has established the motivation, research problem, questions, and primary contributions of this thesis. It has also defined the scope of the study, situating the proposed system as a specialized framework for the semantic aggregation and retrieval of internship listings. By balancing extraction accuracy with computational efficiency, the work addresses the limitations of both rigid lexical systems and high-cost LLM-dependent approaches. The subsequent chapters detail the theoretical foundation, implementation, and empirical evaluation of this architecture.
