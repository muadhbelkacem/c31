\section{Discussion}
This chapter discusses the experimental results presented in the results chapter. It analyzes the failure modes of the extraction and classification modules, discusses trade-offs between system accuracy and computational cost, and identifies limitations and threats to validity according to established methodologies for empirical software and NLP evaluation [7, 36].

\subsection{Error Analysis}
Although the reported results indicate useful performance across extraction and retrieval tasks, qualitative error analysis remains necessary for understanding the challenges of processing semi-structured job data [44, 50].

\subsubsection{Extraction Failures and Implicit Information}
The lower recall for lexical skill extraction highlights the challenge of implicit skill requirements. Many job postings omit foundational skills that are assumed for a role. This semantic gap is a known issue in recruitment informatics, where requirements are often latent rather than explicit [17, 41]. In date extraction, errors primarily occurred in rolling recruitment cycles. The regex-based Layer A often failed to normalize these expressions into precise temporal ranges, which is a common limitation of rule-based Information Extraction systems when linguistic variability is high [18].

\subsubsection{Ambiguity in Semantic Mapping}
The gap between Top-1 and Top-3 accuracy for title normalization reveals the impact of professional ambiguity. Generic titles lack sufficient semantic density for the bi-encoder to map them to a specific occupation without analyzing the full text body. This challenge of entity resolution and disambiguation is central to modern taxonomy alignment research [56, 45].

\subsection{Trade-offs: Accuracy vs Cost}
The design of the tiered architecture was motivated by the need to balance performance with operational feasibility, a core principle in Green AI and efficient deep learning [46, 37].

\subsubsection{Marginal Accuracy and Operational Cost}
While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer strong zero-shot capabilities, marginal accuracy gains may come with substantial latency and financial cost [20, 3]. By delegating most of the workload to local, small-scale models, the system aims to maintain usable accuracy while reducing reliance on external APIs [37].

\subsubsection{Latency and User Experience}
The reported search latency of 45 ms supports a responsive user interface. A system relying on real-time LLM inference for search-time semantic expansion would likely exceed common thresholds for interactive systems [7]. The use of pre-computed HNSW indices and RRF-based fusion is intended to deliver semantic results with efficiency close to traditional lexical search [35, 10].

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
Several factors may affect the generalizability and robustness of this study, as is common in Information Retrieval and machine learning research [36, 51].

\subsubsection{Internal Validity: Annotation Bias}
The evaluation relies on an expert-annotated reference set. Although inter-annotator agreement was reported as high, subjectivity in labeling relevant skills remains a threat, which is a known challenge in creating benchmarks for specialized domains [26, 51].

\subsubsection{External Validity: Generalizability}
The dataset is primarily focused on technology and business sectors within North American and European contexts and is shaped by the ESCO framework. The effectiveness of the system may decrease when applied to different linguistic domains or regions with different occupational structures [28].

\subsubsection{Construct Validity: Metric Selection}
The study uses standard IR metrics such as MAP and NDCG. However, these metrics do not always correlate directly with real-world user satisfaction in job discovery, where factors such as freshness and company reputation may also play important roles [2, 29].

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This thesis has presented an approach to the lightweight semantic structuring of internship listings, addressing the challenges of high-volume, noisy recruitment data. By combining rule-based extraction with small-scale Transformer models, the work shows how a structured knowledge base can be built while reducing dependence on costly and latency-prone LLM-based processing, consistent with the direction of sustainable and efficient AI [46, 37].

\subsection{Summary of Findings}
The reported experimental results support the central hypothesis that a tiered, hybrid architecture can improve semantic alignment while remaining computationally feasible for practical deployment. The key findings are summarized as follows:


\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em,topsep=0.25em]

\item Efficiency and Scalability: The reported throughput suggests that the system can support frequent updates on commodity hardware, supporting the responsiveness required for interactive applications [7, 25].

\item Extraction Accuracy: Deterministic fields were extracted with high reported precision, while semantic mapping of job roles achieved high reported Top-3 accuracy using lightweight bi-encoders.

\item Search Relevance: The hybrid retrieval strategy reportedly outperformed keyword-only search for conceptual queries in the recruitment domain [51, 34].

\item Operational Cost: By using local models for most processing, the system reduces reliance on external APIs and remains consistent with principles of Knowledge Distillation and efficient inference [20, 37].

\end{itemize}
\subsection{Future Directions}
While the current system provides a foundation for internship analytics, several areas remain for future exploration and improvement [50, 29].

\subsubsection{Multilingual Extraction}
A primary limitation is the current focus on English-language listings. Expanding the pipeline to support multilingual extraction using cross-lingual models would be essential for a truly global internship platform, leveraging the multilingual definitions in the ESCO framework [32].

\subsubsection{Better Section Segmentation}
Future work could employ more specialized models to segment job postings into structured blocks such as ``Responsibilities'' and ``Benefits.'' This would allow more granular skill weighting and better context-aware matching [41, 4].

\subsubsection{Self-training and Distillation}
A future extension could implement a continuous self-training loop in which high-confidence labels generated by larger models are used to periodically fine-tune local models. This distillation process would allow the lightweight components to improve gradually without inheriting high latency [20].

\subsubsection{Continuous Monitoring for Taxonomy Drift}
The technical landscape is in constant flux, with new frameworks and job roles emerging frequently. Implementing automated ``drift detection'' would allow the system's semantic schema to stay up-to-date with minimal manual effort, addressing the challenges of ontology evolution in the labor market [48, 49].


\section{Chapter Conclusion}
This chapter has interpreted the reported findings, identified the principal error sources and validity threats, and outlined future work required to strengthen the platform. The thesis concludes that the proposed lightweight semantic pipeline is a practical direction for organizing internship listings, provided that the final comparative evaluation is completed with measured ranking, latency, cost, and external-platform evidence.
