\chapter{Discussion, Conclusion, and Future Work}

\section{Chapter Introduction}

This final chapter discusses the findings presented in Chapter 6 and explains their meaning
for the thesis as a whole. The discussion focuses on what the current evaluation shows about
noisy scraped recruitment data, semantic structuring, internship detection, uncertainty
handling, structured discovery, and the operational limits of the lightweight approach \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, peffers2007design}.

The chapter is organized into four main parts. Section~7.2 interprets the results in relation to
the research questions and discusses the main limitations observed during evaluation.
Section~7.3 addresses ethical, legal, and privacy considerations linked to automated internship
aggregation and classification. Section~7.4 summarizes the thesis findings and presents future
work. Section~7.5 concludes the chapter by restating the overall contribution of the thesis.

The discussion does not introduce new benchmark claims. It remains limited to the evaluation
scope established in Chapter 6: the inspected 300-role sample, the enriched frontend catalog,
the classification comparison, the local frontend measurements, and the stated
methodological boundaries \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, jain1991art}.


\section{Discussion}

The discussion interprets the implemented artifact as a design-science outcome and treats the empirical results as bounded evidence rather than universal proof of system performance \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design, wohlin2012experimentation}.

\subsection{Interpretation of the Evaluation Results}

The results presented in Chapter 6 show that internship aggregation from general recruitment
sources is affected by substantial noise. In the inspected 300-role sample, the records were
not limited to real internships. The sample contained real internships, fake or non-internship
roles, and ambiguous cases. This confirms the central problem of the thesis: scraped
recruitment data cannot be safely pushed directly into an internship discovery interface
without filtering, normalization, and semantic enrichment \cite{batini2009methodologies}.

This finding directly supports RQ1. The problem is not only that recruitment data has
inconsistent fields or missing metadata. The deeper issue is that many sources mix internships
with full-time jobs, senior roles, generic employment advertisements, trainee-style roles, and
unclear postings. As a result, internship discovery requires more than keyword matching. It
requires a representation strategy that can separate relevant internship opportunities from
unrelated or uncertain records.

The enriched catalog results support RQ2 by showing that the pipeline can transform noisy
postings into structured records suitable for application use. The system produces internship
records with normalized fields, extracted skills, salary information where available, links,
descriptions, internship probability values, and review-related indicators. These outputs show
the practical role of combining deterministic normalization with controlled skill
representation and internship detection. The importance of this result is not only technical. It
shows that heterogeneous recruitment data can be converted into a more consistent
representation for internship-oriented retrieval \cite{doan2012principles, batini2009methodologies, manning2008introduction}.

The frontend search, filtering, and response results support RQ3 within a limited scope. They
show that enriched records can support fast structured discovery under the tested local
conditions. However, these results are not interpreted as proof of formal user
satisfaction, because no user study was conducted. The appropriate conclusion is that the
enriched catalog supports efficient search and filtering behavior, not that the system has been
fully validated as a user experience.

The classification comparison supports RQ4. Human review identified 128 real internships, 9
ambiguous cases, and 163 fake or non-internship roles in the 300-role sample.
ChatGPT5.5-assisted review achieved sample-level agreement with the human review on this
inspected sample. The Transformer achieved strong binary internship detection results, but it
did not produce an ambiguous category. This distinction is important because ambiguity
is not always forced into a real/fake decision. Some cases require review because the
available posting information is incomplete, unclear, or open to interpretation \cite{sokolova2009systematic, krippendorff2004content}.

Finally, the operational measurements support RQ5 by showing that the lightweight approach
is feasible within the tested environment. The build results, file-size results, frontend
response measurements, and search/filter timing results indicate that the system can support
structured discovery without requiring heavy runtime inference in the frontend. At the same
time, these results are bounded by the local test setup and are not generalized to all
deployment environments \cite{jain1991art, schwartz2020green}.


\subsection{Error Analysis and Ambiguity Handling}

The main source of difficulty observed in the evaluation is ambiguity in recruitment
language. Some postings clearly describe internships through explicit terms such as intern,
internship, co-op, placement, or stagiaire. Other postings are clearly unrelated because they
refer to senior, manager, permanent, or general professional roles. The difficult cases lie
between these two groups \cite{krippendorff2004content}.

Ambiguous roles may include trainee programs, graduate programs, student-oriented jobs,
apprenticeships, temporary training roles, or postings that use unclear employer-specific
language. In these cases, the title alone may not be enough to determine whether the role
is treated as a real internship. The description, duration, target audience, and
educational context may be necessary, but these details are not always available or complete.

This explains why the Transformer result is interpreted carefully. The Transformer
performs binary classification, separating roles into real internship and fake/non-internship
classes. Because it has no ambiguous output, it must force borderline records into one of the
two classes. This is useful for fast detection, but it is less suitable for cases where the correct
decision is delayed until review. Therefore, the existence of the 9 ambiguous cases in
the inspected sample is not a weakness of the evaluation. It is an important finding about the
nature of internship data \cite{sokolova2009systematic, krippendorff2004content}.

The human and ChatGPT5.5-assisted review comparison also supports this interpretation.
ChatGPT5.5-assisted review matched the human distribution on the inspected sample, but
this is described only as sample-level agreement. It does not prove universal
correctness. Its value in the thesis is that it provides a review-support perspective for
ambiguous cases, while the Transformer provides a lightweight binary detection mechanism.

A second limitation concerns incomplete or inconsistent metadata. Some postings contain
missing links, sparse descriptions, unclear locations, or salary fields that are unavailable or
inconsistently formatted. The pipeline can normalize and structure the information that is
present, but it cannot fully recover information that was not captured or was not expressed
clearly in the source. This reinforces the need for cautious claims about the system. The
pipeline improves structure and discovery readiness, but it does not eliminate all uncertainty
from noisy recruitment data \cite{batini2009methodologies, doan2012principles}.

\subsection{Trade-offs Between Detection, Structure, and Local Feasibility}

The proposed architecture reflects a trade-off between semantic capability, explainability, and
lightweight operation. A purely keyword-based approach would be simple and transparent,
but it would struggle with varied internship terminology and employer-specific wording. A
fully generative or heavyweight model-based approach could provide stronger contextual
reasoning in some cases, but it would introduce more complexity and dependence on external
inference. The thesis therefore adopts a middle position \cite{bass2012software, schwartz2020green, jain1991art}.

Deterministic normalization is used where rules are appropriate and explainable. This
includes field cleaning, schema consistency, stable representation, and structured frontend
preparation. Lightweight Transformer-based detection is used where semantic interpretation
is helpful, especially for distinguishing real internships from noisy scraped roles. Manual or
ChatGPT5.5-assisted review is reserved for cases where automatic classification is less
reliable \cite{sarawagi2008information, krippendorff2004content}.

This layered design is important because it avoids treating all parts of the problem as if they
require the same solution. Some tasks are best handled through rules, some through semantic
classification, and some through review. The result is a system that remains interpretable
while still benefiting from lightweight semantic modeling.

The local frontend evidence also reflects this trade-off. By processing records before frontend
consumption, the application can use a structured JSON catalog for browsing, searching, and
filtering. This keeps expensive processing outside the live user-facing path and supports
responsive interaction under the tested local conditions. However, this design also means that
catalog quality depends on the offline enrichment process and on the quality of the scraped
input records \cite{jain1991art, kleppmann2017designing}.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

Several threats to validity affect the interpretation of the results. The first is sample size.
The classification evaluation is based on 300 inspected roles. This sample is useful for thesis
evaluation, but it does not represent every recruitment platform, country, language, industry,
or internship type. Broader validation would require larger and more diverse samples \cite{wohlin2012experimentation}.

The second threat concerns the construction of the reference labels. Human review is treated
as the reference for the 300-role sample, but internship classification can involve judgment,
especially for borderline cases. The presence of ambiguous labels helps address this issue, but
it does not remove the subjectivity of classification decisions \cite{krippendorff2004content}.

The third threat concerns ChatGPT5.5-assisted review. Its agreement with human review
is understood as agreement on the inspected sample, not as a general proof that
ChatGPT5.5 will always classify internships correctly. This limitation is important for
avoiding overclaiming.

The fourth threat concerns the Transformer evaluation. Since the Transformer produces only
binary outputs, it cannot represent ambiguity directly. This makes it useful for real/fake
internship detection, but less expressive than the human review framework. Future versions
should consider adding a confidence band or an explicit review-needed class.

The fifth threat concerns frontend and operational measurements. The response-time, build,
file-size, and search/filter results were observed under local test conditions. They support
local feasibility, but they do not guarantee identical performance under different hardware,
network, hosting, caching, or production traffic conditions \cite{jain1991art}.

The sixth threat concerns user validation. The frontend results show structured discovery
behavior and fast filtering, but no formal user study was conducted with students. Therefore,
the thesis does not claim proven user satisfaction or complete usability validation. A
user-centered evaluation would be required to make stronger claims about real-world
usefulness \cite{wohlin2012experimentation}.



\section{Ethical, Legal, and Privacy Considerations}

Ethical interpretation is necessary because automated recruitment-related systems can affect visibility, access, and trust even when they are used for opportunity discovery rather than candidate selection \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, rigotti2024fairness}.

\subsection{Data Privacy and Responsible Data Use}

The system processes recruitment postings rather than private student profiles or automated
hiring decisions. This reduces some privacy risks, but it does not remove the need for
responsible data handling. Scraped or externally collected job data may include employer
names, locations, links, and role descriptions. These records are handled in a way that
respects source terms, avoids unnecessary personal data collection, and preserves traceability
to the original posting where possible \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, rigotti2024fairness}.

The thesis does not propose automated candidate ranking, hiring decisions, or student
profiling. This is an important ethical boundary. The system is designed to organize
internship opportunities for discovery, not to decide which students deserve opportunities.
Maintaining this boundary reduces the risk of harmful automated decision-making \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics}.

\subsection{Bias and Representation}

Internship discovery systems can reflect the limitations of their source data. If the scraped
sources overrepresent certain platforms, regions, employers, languages, or industries, the
resulting catalog will also reflect those imbalances. This may affect which opportunities are
visible to users and which categories appear more common \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, rigotti2024fairness}.

The semantic enrichment pipeline can improve structure and filtering, but it cannot fully
correct source-level bias by itself. Responsible deployment would require monitoring the
diversity of sources, reviewing category coverage, and ensuring that the system does not
present a narrow or incomplete corpus as a complete view of the internship market.

Ambiguous-case handling also has fairness implications. Forcing uncertain roles into a
real/fake classification may incorrectly exclude valid opportunities or include irrelevant ones.
Preserving review indicators helps reduce this risk by making uncertainty visible rather than
hiding it behind overconfident automation \cite{krippendorff2004content, rigotti2024fairness}.

\subsection{Transparency and Explainability}

Transparency is important because internship discovery affects student access to
opportunities. The system is designed to make its processing logic understandable where possible.
Deterministic normalization, controlled skills, categories, source links, and review indicators
all support a more explainable architecture than an opaque end-to-end model \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, bass2012software}.

However, the current system does not fully expose explanations to end users. Future
deployment could improve transparency by showing why a role was classified as an
internship, why it was flagged for review, which skills were extracted, and which metadata
was missing or uncertain. Such explanation features would make the system easier to audit
and more trustworthy in educational or career-support contexts \cite{rigotti2024fairness}.


\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This thesis has presented a lightweight semantic enrichment pipeline for transforming noisy
scraped recruitment data into structured internship records. The system combines
deterministic normalization, controlled metadata representation, internship detection, review
indicators, and frontend-ready JSON storage.
The purpose of this design is to support
internship-oriented discovery while preserving interpretability and manageable complexity \cite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design, batini2009methodologies}.

The current evaluation supports the feasibility and usefulness of this approach within its
tested scope.
It shows that scraped recruitment data includes real internships, fake or
non-internship roles, and ambiguous cases; that the pipeline can structure accepted records for
frontend use; that the Transformer performs strongly as a binary detector; and that enriched
records can support fast local search and filtering \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, sokolova2009systematic, jain1991art}.


\subsection{Summary of Findings}

For RQ1, the evaluation confirms that heterogeneous internship data contains structural,
lexical, semantic, and quality-related inconsistencies. The inspected 300-role sample
demonstrates that scraped recruitment sources include a mixture of valid internships,
unrelated roles, and ambiguous cases. This supports the thesis claim that internship discovery
is a semantic data-representation problem, not only a search-interface problem \cite{batini2009methodologies}.

For RQ2, the results show that rule-based normalization, controlled skill representation,
internship probabilities, and review indicators can be combined into a coherent enrichment
model. The enriched frontend catalog demonstrates that noisy postings can be transformed
into structured records suitable for browsing, filtering, and inspection \cite{doan2012principles, batini2009methodologies}.

For RQ3, the frontend evaluation shows that enriched records can support structured
discovery behavior through search, filtering, and fast response under local test conditions.
The finding is interpreted as support for structured discovery quality, not as formal
proof of user satisfaction \cite{manning2008introduction, jain1991art}.

For RQ4, the comparison between human review, ChatGPT5.5-assisted review, and
Transformer detection shows that internship classification benefits from both automation and
review. The Transformer provides strong binary detection, while ambiguous cases remain
better handled through review or explicit uncertainty indicators \cite{sokolova2009systematic, krippendorff2004content}.

For RQ5, the local build, file-size, response-time, and search/filter results show that the
lightweight approach is operationally feasible within the evaluated environment. However,
the results remain bounded by the sample size, local test conditions, and absence of formal
user validation \cite{jain1991art, schwartz2020green, wohlin2012experimentation}.

Overall, the thesis supports the claim that lightweight semantic enrichment can improve the
organization and discovery readiness of noisy internship data while preserving transparency
and practical deployment simplicity \cite{schwartz2020green, bass2012software}.

\subsection{Future Directions}

Several future directions can strengthen this work. First, the classification evaluation should
be expanded beyond the 300-role sample. A larger dataset with multiple platforms, regions,
and domains would improve confidence in the generalizability of the results. Independent
human annotators could also be used to measure agreement and reduce subjectivity in the
reference labels \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, krippendorff2004content}.

Second, future work should improve ambiguity handling. The Transformer currently
performs binary classification, but the evaluation shows that some records are better treated
as uncertain. A future model could include a third class, confidence thresholds, or a
review-needed flag to better match the human inspection framework \cite{sokolova2009systematic, krippendorff2004content}.

Third, the system should be evaluated with real users. A student-centered study could
examine whether enriched metadata, skill filters, categories, and review indicators actually
improve discovery behavior in practice. Such a study would allow stronger claims about
usability and usefulness \cite{wohlin2012experimentation}.

Fourth, the corpus should be expanded and diversified. More internship-specific sources,
university career portals, employer pages, multilingual postings, and regional platforms
would improve coverage and reduce dependence on a narrow set of sources \cite{batini2009methodologies}.

Fifth, skill extraction and metadata normalization can be improved. Future versions could
better distinguish required and preferred skills, handle implicit skills, detect missing or
uncertain salary information, and normalize multilingual or employer-specific terminology
more effectively \cite{staab2009handbook, shvaiko2013ontology}.

Finally, deployment testing should be extended beyond the local environment. Cloud
deployment, different hardware, network variation, caching behavior, and larger catalog sizes
should be evaluated before making broader operational claims \cite{jain1991art, bass2012software}.

\subsection{Practical Implications for Deployment}

The thesis suggests that a lightweight enrichment architecture can be useful for
student-centered internship discovery systems. Instead of relying on raw scraped records, a
platform can process postings before publication, normalize their fields, detect internship
relevance, extract useful metadata, and flag uncertain cases for review \cite{batini2009methodologies, doan2012principles}.

This approach is practical because it separates offline enrichment from frontend interaction.
The frontend can operate over a structured catalog, while the more complex cleaning and
classification work happens before records are served to users. This supports fast browsing
and filtering under the tested local conditions \cite{jain1991art, kleppmann2017designing}.

The approach is also explainable. Rules, extracted metadata, links, and review indicators
make the system easier to inspect than a fully opaque model. This is especially important in
academic, educational, or public-service contexts where users and administrators may need to
understand why a record was accepted, rejected, or flagged \cite{hunkenschroer2022ethics, rigotti2024fairness}.

At the same time, practical deployment should preserve human oversight. The system should
not pretend that every internship classification decision is certain. Ambiguous records should
remain visible as review cases, especially when the available source information is
incomplete or unclear \cite{krippendorff2004content}.


\section{Chapter Conclusion}

This chapter interpreted the results of Chapter 6 in relation to the thesis research questions. It
explained that the main challenge in internship aggregation is not only technical processing,
but the semantic uncertainty and noise found in scraped recruitment data. The discussion
showed that the proposed pipeline can structure noisy records, support internship detection,
preserve ambiguous cases for review, and enable efficient frontend discovery within the
tested local environment \cite{peffers2007design, wohlin2012experimentation}.

The chapter also identified the main limitations of the work: the 300-role evaluation sample,
the binary nature of the Transformer output, the sample-level interpretation of ChatGPT5.5
agreement, the local scope of frontend measurements, and the absence of a formal user study.
These limitations define the boundaries of the findings without removing their value \cite{wohlin2012experimentation, jain1991art}.

Future work should therefore focus on larger and more diverse evaluation samples, stronger
ambiguous-case handling, real student-centered validation, expanded source coverage,
improved skill and metadata extraction, and deployment testing beyond the local
environment \cite{krippendorff2004content, batini2009methodologies}.

Overall, this thesis concludes that lightweight semantic enrichment is a defensible and
practical approach for transforming heterogeneous recruitment data into structured,
interpretable, and discovery-ready internship records. The contribution lies not only in the
implemented system, but in showing how normalization, semantic detection, uncertainty
handling, and frontend-ready representation can be combined into a coherent internship
discovery pipeline \cite{hevner2004design, doan2012principles, schwartz2020green}.
